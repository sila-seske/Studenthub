How to write a first CV for an internship

You do not need a job history to write a good CV for an internship. Recruiters look for clear information, relevant coursework and evidence that you finish what you start. This guide shows you how to present what you already have.

Last updated 14 November 2026

What recruiters look for

Recruiters often scan a CV quickly. In that first scan they want to know who you are, what you are studying, what you can do, and whether you have built or finished anything.

The sections to include

  1. Contact details: full name, phone number, professional email address, and a link to your portfolio or GitHub if you have one.
  2. A short profile: two or three lines about what you study and what you want to do.
  3. Education: your university, programme, expected graduation year and, if it is strong, your grade or CGPA with the scale.
  4. Projects or coursework: the most relevant work, with a clear description of what you did.
  5. Skills: programs, tools and languages you can use, grouped by type.
  6. Activities: student societies, volunteering, or roles you held in a club.

Turn coursework into experience

Coursework is experience if you describe it well. Say what you built or solved, who it was for, and what the result was. Numbers help, even small ones.

Weak bulletStronger bullet
Made a timetable app for a class projectBuilt a timetable app with reminders for 40 classmates, tested on Android and Chrome
Helped with a group assignmentLed a group of four to finish a database design, wrote the queries and presented the result
Good at Microsoft ExcelBuilt a budget spreadsheet with formulas and charts that tracked monthly spending

Keep it to one page

For a first internship, one page is the right length. Use a clear font at around 10 to 11 points, and keep the spacing the same throughout. Save and send the CV as a PDF so the layout does not change on the recipient's screen.

Tailor it for each application

Read the advertisement carefully and note the skills it asks for. If you have those skills, use the same words in your profile or skills section. Only claim skills you can actually show.

A checklist before you send it

  • Spelling and grammar checked by someone else.
  • A professional email address.
  • A clear file name, such as Firstname-Lastname-CV.pdf.
  • Every link tested and working.
  • Dates in one format, used throughout.

What to leave out

  • Long objective statements that describe what you hope to gain. Describe what you offer instead.
  • Personal details that are not needed for the role, such as religion, ethnicity, or marital status.
  • Hobbies that have nothing to do with the work, unless they show a skill the employer wants.

Frequently asked questions

I have no projects. What can I add?

Coursework, group assignments, volunteering, roles in a student society, and online courses with a certificate all count. Describe what you did in the same clear way.

Should I include my CGPA?

Include it if it is strong, or if the advertisement asks for it. Always state the scale, for example 4.2 out of 5.0. If your grade is weak, leave it out and focus on the other sections.

Do I need a cover letter?

Write one if the advertisement asks for it, or if the application form has a space for one. Keep it to one page and mention the specific role and why you are interested in it.

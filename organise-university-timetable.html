How to organise a university timetable

To organise a university timetable, enter every required session with its day, start time, end time and room, then check the list for clashes before you add anything else. Use the official timetable from your department as the source, and review it every week.

Last updated 4 November 2026

What you need before you start

Have the official timetable from your department or student portal, the course code for each module and the room for each session. Optional sessions, such as a tutorial you may attend, belong in a separate list so they do not hide the required ones.

Step 1: Enter every required session

  1. Write each session as course code, day, start time, end time and room.
  2. Use one time format throughout, such as 24-hour times, so sessions are easy to compare.
  3. Include practicals and tutorials, not only lectures.

Step 2: Check for clashes

A clash happens when two required sessions overlap on the same day. Compare the start and end times for each day. A session that ends at 11:00 does not clash with one that starts at 11:00, but any real overlap needs a decision. Ask your department which session takes priority, and whether a missed session can be made up.

StudentHub marks overlapping classes in red in the weekly timetable, so clashes are easy to spot.

Step 3: Add exams and deadlines

Exam dates belong on the same timeline as your classes. Add the course code, date, time and venue for each exam, and set reminders early enough to reach the venue. Assignment deadlines work the same way.

Step 4: Review it every week

Choose a fixed time each week, such as Sunday evening, to check your timetable against the latest notices from your department. Timetables change after registration, and a change you miss is a common cause of a missed class.

What this guide does not cover

This guide does not explain course registration or how your university allocates rooms. For any clash or change, confirm with your department, because only they can approve a change.

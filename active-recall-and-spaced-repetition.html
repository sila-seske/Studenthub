Study methods

Active recall and spaced repetition

Re-reading your notes feels productive, but it is one of the weakest ways to remember. Two methods work far better: testing yourself, which is called active recall, and reviewing across several days, which is called spaced repetition.

Last updated 14 November 2026

Why re-reading is not enough

A page you have just read feels familiar, so it feels known. Familiarity is not the same as being able to recall something when you need it, such as in an exam room.

A widely cited review of study techniques by Dunlosky and colleagues (2013) rated practice testing and spreading out study sessions as high-utility methods. Re-reading and highlighting were rated low.

Active recall: close the book and answer

After you finish a section, close your notes and write everything you remember about it. Then open your notes and check what you missed. The missing parts are the ones to review.

  • For definitions, explain the idea in your own words, without looking.
  • For calculations, solve the problem from a blank page.
  • For lists of facts, write them from memory and count how many you got.

Write questions on index cards: the question on the front, the answer on the back. Reading the question and saying the answer aloud is the recall step.

Spaced repetition: review at growing gaps

Spaced repetition means reviewing a topic again after a gap, then after a longer gap, and so on. A common pattern is one day, three days, a week, two weeks and a month. If you forget a card, move it back to a shorter gap.

You do not need an app. Use three piles of cards: Today, This week and Later. Answer the cards in Today. Correct answers move to This week or Later. Wrong answers stay in Today.

A weekly schedule

Plan two or three short sessions of about 30 minutes each day. Each session should do two things: recall what you studied recently, and learn something new.

DayWhat you do
MondayLearn a new section, then write recall questions for it
TuesdayAnswer the cards in Today, then learn the next section
WednesdayAnswer the cards in Today and This week
ThursdayLearn a new section, then add its cards to the piles
FridayAnswer the cards in all piles and write a short summary of the week

Mix topics in the same session

Studying one topic for a whole session feels smooth, but mixing two or three subjects in the same session usually helps you remember more. It feels harder while you are doing it, which is a sign that the practice is working.

Common mistakes

  • Writing cards that are too long. Keep each card to one idea.
  • Reviewing only the cards you already know, because they feel good. Spend your time on the cards you get wrong.
  • Skipping review days when you are busy. A short review is better than none, so do a five-minute version.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a flashcard app?

No. Paper cards work well. An app becomes useful when you have hundreds of cards and need reminders for each review date.

How many cards should I do each day?

Keep to a number you can finish in 20 to 30 minutes. If you cannot finish, reduce the number of new cards, not the reviews.

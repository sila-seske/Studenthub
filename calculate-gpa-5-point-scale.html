How to calculate your GPA on a 5-point scale

To calculate your GPA on a 5-point scale, multiply each course’s grade point by its credit units, add those results together, then divide by the total units. Your institution’s regulations decide the official figure, so treat your result as a planning estimate.

The formula

GPA = (grade points × units, added for every course) ÷ total units.

A worked example

Suppose a semester has three courses: MTH 101 (3 units, grade A), PHY 102 (3 units, grade B) and CSC 201 (2 units, grade C).

  1. MTH 101: 5 × 3 = 15
  2. PHY 102: 4 × 3 = 12
  3. CSC 201: 3 × 2 = 6

Total points are 33 and total units are 8. The GPA is 33 ÷ 8 = 4.125, which rounds to 4.13.

What the GPA means

Many universities use bands like these. Check your handbook for the exact cut-offs.

Cumulative GPA

A cumulative GPA (CGPA) uses the same method across every course you have taken. Add the points and units for the whole programme. Do not simply average your semester GPAs, because semesters can carry different unit totals.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting to multiply each grade point by its units.
  • Dividing by the number of courses instead of the total units.
  • Rounding early. Keep two decimal places until the final result.

What this guide does not cover

The results here are estimates. For an application or an official record, use your result slip or transcript.

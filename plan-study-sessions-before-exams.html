How to plan study sessions before exams

To plan study sessions before exams, list each exam with its date, rate how confident you feel about each subject, then schedule short, focused sessions across the days you have. Start with the subjects you know least, and finish your main revision at least a day before each exam.

Last updated 4 November 2026

Start with a list of exams

Write down each exam with its date, time and venue. Count the days between today and each exam. That count sets the budget for your plan, and everything else follows from it.

Rate each subject

Give each subject a confidence score from 1 to 5. Subjects scored 1 or 2 need the most sessions. Confidence is an estimate, so update the scores after each session.

Build sessions around your classes

Place sessions in the gaps between classes, at the time of day when you concentrate best. A focused block of 45 to 60 minutes followed by a short break is a common starting point. Shorter sessions are easier to keep when your timetable is busy.

Mix subjects across the day

Switching subjects gives each one a rest and stops one hard topic from taking over the whole day. Avoid spending a full day on a subject you already know well.

Practise, then review

  1. Work through past questions under timed conditions.
  2. Mark what you got wrong, and review those items the next day.
  3. Return to your weakest subject before the exam, not only at the start of your plan.

Example: three exams in ten days

Suppose you have exams in 4, 7 and 10 days. Give your lowest-rated subject two sessions a day for the first few days, then one a day until the day before its exam. Keep the other subjects at one session a day, and leave the day before each exam for light review.

What this guide does not cover

A plan does not replace sleep, meals or rest, and it cannot guarantee a result. Adjust it whenever your classes or assessments change.

A study routine that costs nothing

You do not need a special planner, a paid app or a new desk to study well. A good routine uses your timetable, a notebook or your phone's notes app, and a few habits that cost nothing.

Last updated 14 November 2026

Start with your timetable

Write down your classes, work, travel and meals for one week. Look at the gaps. Mark two or three of them as study blocks, and treat them as fixed appointments.

Set a target you can finish

"Study chemistry" is not a target. "Revise section 3 and answer ten questions" is. Each study block should have one clear output you can check when it ends.

Use the tools you already have

  • Your phone's notes app or a notebook for summaries and lists.
  • A timer on your phone for study blocks and breaks.
  • The library, which usually has reading tables, quiet areas and past question files.
  • Past questions from your course representative or classmates.
  • Free lecture material your lecturers have published, read within the terms they set.

Study where you can focus

Choose one place for most of your study, such as a library corner or a quiet table at home. When you sit in the same place, your mind starts to switch into study mode more quickly.

If the place is noisy, plan the hardest work for the quietest time of the day, and keep easier tasks, such as reading notes, for busier times.

Protect your energy

  • Sleep at a steady time. Memory is consolidated during sleep.
  • Drink water and take a short walk between blocks.
  • Stop studying at a set time each night, so that you do not start cramming when you are tired.

Review once a week

Spend 20 minutes on Sunday looking at what got done, what did not, and why. Move unfinished tasks to the next week, and change one habit that did not work.

Keep spending out of the way

Share books with classmates, print only the pages you need, and use the library's reservation system where it exists. Buying every book or tool is a cost you do not need to carry to pass your courses.

Common mistakes

  • Planning every hour of the day. Leave some space, or the plan will break on the first busy day.
  • Spending time on tools and apps instead of studying.
  • Only studying the subject you enjoy. Give the harder subjects a fixed block each week.

Frequently asked questions

Is a paper planner better than a phone?

Either works. Use the one you open every day. A plan that you never look at does not help, however good it is.

How long should a study block be?

Start with 45 minutes and a 10-minute break, then adjust. If you finish the target early, stop. If you cannot finish, make the next target smaller.

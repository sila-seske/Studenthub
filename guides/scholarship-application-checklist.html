A checklist for scholarship applications

Scholarship applications are won by students who prepare early and follow the rules exactly. This checklist covers the steps from finding an opportunity to sending your application. It does not list specific awards, because rules and deadlines change. Always confirm details on the official source.

Last updated 14 November 2026

Find and verify before you apply

  • Use only the official website of the funder, or the notice board of your institution.
  • Be cautious of anyone who asks you to pay to apply, or who promises that a place is guaranteed.
  • Note the deadline, the eligibility rules and the documents required before you start writing.

Prepare documents early

Most applications ask for the same things. Gather them before you start a form, so that a missing document does not stop you on the last day.

  • Academic transcript or result slip.
  • Proof of enrolment or an admission letter.
  • Means of identification, such as a national ID or passport.
  • Recommendation letters. Ask at least three to four weeks before the deadline.
  • A personal statement, which you should write once and then adapt for each application.
  • Scanned PDF copies with clear file names.

Write a personal statement that answers the question

Answer the exact question you are given. Use one clear example from your life or your studies to show what you have done, then explain what you will do with the award.

Read the statement aloud before you submit it. Ask someone you trust to read it too. Keep to the word limit, because a statement that goes over may be cut or rejected.

Ask for recommendation letters early

Give each referee the deadline, your draft statement and a short list of your achievements. Thank them after you submit, and tell them the result when you know it.

Final checks before you submit

  • Every field is completed, including the optional ones if they help your case.
  • Each file is in the requested format and under the size limit.
  • You have kept a copy of everything you sent.
  • You submit before the deadline, not on it. Save the confirmation email or a screenshot.

Common mistakes

  • Missing one document and losing the whole application.
  • Sending a statement written for a different scholarship.
  • Applying without checking eligibility, then wasting time on a form you cannot submit.
  • Trusting unofficial posts about deadlines or forms.

After you apply

Keep every email from the funder. Reply quickly if they ask for more information. If you are not successful, ask for feedback where it is offered, and apply again in the next cycle with a stronger statement.

Frequently asked questions

Can I apply for more than one scholarship?

Usually yes, unless a rule says otherwise. Check each set of rules, and keep a separate copy of each application.

What if I do not meet every requirement?

If you meet the main criteria, apply and then read the notes carefully. If you are unsure, ask the funder before the deadline.

How can I tell a scholarship is genuine?

It is listed on the official website of the funder or your institution, it describes how to apply, and it never asks you to pay to apply.
